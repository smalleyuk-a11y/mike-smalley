Case Studies Mike Smalley
Brand: LuckyBlock

Casino Sign-Up

Redesigning the LuckyBlock sign-up flow to eliminate friction points, streamline player onboarding, and optimize welcome offer conversion.

Role

Product Design

Tools

Figma, Hotjar, Google Analytics, Lyssna

Timeframe

6 weeks

My Role

  • Conducted competitive benchmarking and user data analysis to identify sign-up friction points
  • Mapped out user flows and detailed wireframes to optimise step-by-step conversion
  • Designed user interfaces applying design system standards for brand consistency and accessibility
  • Ran usability testing on prototypes via Lyssna to validate user comprehension and refine features
  • Iterated on post-test feedback by designing a deposit popup to boost offer clarity and player conversion

The Problem

High drop-off rates during the LuckyBlock sign-up flow were preventing potential players from reaching the lobby, impacting user acquisition. My goal was to identify the key points of friction in the onboarding experience, redesign the flow to improve completion rates, and better highlight our welcome offer to drive conversion.

Research & Insights

Analysing data from our existing flow showed that drop-off was mainly driven by username creation and confusing password validation requirements. Asking users to fill out four fields upfront created an immediate barrier.

I benchmarked competitor platforms and found they offered frictionless entry via SSO, required fewer initial fields, and kept user bonuses visible from step one. While some platforms avoided mandatory usernames altogether, which was not an option for our system, I focused on optimising the surrounding experience to minimise friction wherever possible.

Ideation & Strategy

Once the issues were understood, I created a user flow to map out the iterations, aiming to solve the problems we discovered. This included introducing SSO so users can bypass most requirements, as well as adding auto username suggestions to let them quickly bypass that step, too. I choose to integrate Google and MetaMask SSO directly into step one of the flow due to their popularity in the sector.

The flow was then mapped out in more detail using wireframes, finding the best way to showcase the offer, making sure field requirements were permanently visible and error messaging was helpful. I also collapsed the Referral Code field since it was rarely used, further reducing cognitive load. The login flow also needed fixing due to the new sign methods and visual presentation. The wireframe showed it made sense to move the username creation, referral, checkboxes to page two to reduce cognitive overload on the first page.

Design & Testing

I created the UI by applying the design system to ensure brand consistency and accessibility compliance. I tested the prototype on 8 participants via Lyssna to evaluate flow comprehension and friction. This highlighted an issue where some users were unsure if the offer was active or missed due to not using the referral code, which wasn't required for the offer. To solve this, I added an end-of-flow deposit pop-up so users can grab their reward, which has the added bonus of converting users to active players.

Another user commented it was odd that the username can only be autogenerated and not written. This will be solved in the live version since the text will be immediately selected, showing it's editable.

Outcome

The A/B test was a success, delivering an 11% boost in conversion and streamlining the onboarding flow. We planned a follow-up test comparing a single-column layout against our two-step process to optimize further, but deprioritized it to focus on higher-priority product features.

What I Learnt

Mandatory usernames were a non-negotiable backend requirement, but they didn't have to be a barrier. When you can’t eliminate a required step, you can still eliminate the friction around it in this case, using auto-suggestions to make it effortless.
