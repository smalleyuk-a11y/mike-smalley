Milk&More

Frictionless Grocery Onboarding

Milk & More is a popular UK grocery delivery service built around convenience and fresh produce. Friendly neighbourhood milkmen deliver customers’ orders, either as one-offs or as subscriptions.

Team

Product Design (me), Product Manager, Engineer

Tools

Figma, Mixpanel, Lyssna

Timeframe

11 Weeks

View live Play Store

The Problem

Because product availability depends on postcode, the app’s login and registration wall was a particular problem for new users. The only postcode lookup sat inside a long sign-up form, so users couldn’t find out whether the service reached them until they had completed it. Browsing products and understanding the offer were locked behind the same wall.

Research & Insights

I led the app redesign and, following launch, drove multiple rounds of optimisation across key areas of the experience, including this project.

Previous Sign-up or Sign-in page

Sign-up form

  • 89% of users dropped out on the registration screen before seeing any products
  • Users only learned whether the service covered their postcode after a sign-up of 2-3 minutes, so those outside the delivery area invested time in an account they could never use
  • App Store reviews and support tickets raised the same frustration with the registration flow
  • The form itself added friction. Screen recordings showed users opening the sign-up form, reviewing it, and leaving; strict password requirements added significant friction at registration.
  • The service's key benefits weren't communicated early enough, so users had little reason or trust to create an account

I also reviewed the personas and audited other UK grocery apps to see how they handled onboarding and login. The personas were developed from insights gathered during stakeholder interviews, which helped identify key user needs, behaviours and pain points.

Ideation & Strategy

Wireframe

Amended user flow

The goal was to remove the registration barrier while still guiding users towards sign-up and purchase. I worked with the engineer to confirm the postcode lookup could run on the first screen without an account, and with the PM to agree browse-first as the priority.

  1. Immediate postcode lookup built into the first screen. Users can check availability without filling in a form.
  2. Browse first, register at purchase intent. Users can explore the store, categories, product pages and pricing freely. Registration is only prompted when they confirm their basket and move to purchase.

Design & Testing

I built the final UI using the existing design system and created a clickable prototype covering onboarding, browsing and registration. I tested it through Lyssna with 8 participants. The testing was qualitative, focused on whether users understood the service, could navigate the new journey and knew what was required at key points.

Password requirements
An animation meant to explain the requirements caused confusion. I replaced it with simpler, clearer feedback.

Clearer password feedback

Delivery schedules
Users weren't clear which days their local delivery person operated. I made the schedule explicit.

Explicit delivery schedule

Outcome

The new onboarding shipped in the live Android app, and I supported the team through implementation.

  • Sign-up abandonment fell from 89% to 74%, a 15 percentage point reduction, measured in Mixpanel over 3 months after release.
  • Users now find out whether the service covers their postcode in less than 10 seconds, down from 2-3 minutes based on testing
  • Users can explore products and check availability before being asked to register

What I Learnt

Users are more willing to commit once they've experienced some value. When you ask people to register can matter as much as how you design it.
