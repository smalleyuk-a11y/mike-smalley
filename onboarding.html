Milk&More

Grocery Onboarding

Milk & More is a UK grocery box delivery service focused on convenience, fresh produce and sustainable living. I was the sole UI/UX designer on a broader mobile app redesign, including the onboarding and product discovery experience.

Role

Product Design

Tools

Figma, UXCam, Mixpanel, Lyssna

Timeframe

2 months

My Role

  • Analysed user data and feedback to identify onboarding friction
  • Worked with stakeholders to define user personas and needs
  • Mapped user journeys and created wireframes
  • Designed the onboarding and browsing experience using our design system
  • Designed the store, category pages and product detail pages
  • Tested the prototype with 8 participants using Lyssna
  • Iterated on findings and delivered a 14% reduction in sign-up abandonment

The Problem

The app opened with a login and registration screen, giving new users no opportunity to explore the product first. Unlike typical grocery apps, Milk & More relies on postcodes upfront due to area-dependent product availability.

Users couldn't browse products, understand the service or check delivery availability without registering. I needed to remove this barrier while still guiding users towards registration and purchase.

Research & Insights

I reviewed analytics, user feedback and the existing journey to understand where users were dropping out and what prevented them from exploring the service.

The key insight was that users were being asked to commit before they had experienced the product. They couldn't browse products, understand the service or check availability without first creating an account. I also identified:

  • Availability varied by postcode, but users only discovered this during registration
  • The service's key benefits weren't being communicated early enough
  • Strict password requirements added unnecessary friction to registration
  • Users needed more information to build trust before committing to an account

I also worked with stakeholders to map our primary user personas, highlighting the importance of convenience, free delivery and eco-friendly sourcing. This led to three principles: show value, reduce effort and build trust.

Ideation & Strategy

Wireframe

User flow

I redesigned the journey around a browse-first experience. The new onboarding carousel introduced the service through feature-led slides, using the space to communicate key benefits. The postcode lookup was built into the carousel, allowing users to check availability without creating an account.

Onboarding left, Login right

Users could then browse the store, categories and product detail pages without registering. Registration was introduced when users showed purchase intent. For example, a user could explore a product and its pricing, but selecting the purchase action would prompt them to complete registration.

Onboarding → Postcode → Browse → Product → Purchase intent → Registration → Purchase

If the service is active in their area, users see a confirmation page featuring a local milkman profile to build trust and familiarity. This page also displays delivery schedules alongside calls to action to browse the shop or complete sign-up. If the service is not yet active, users can join a waitlist instead.

I also split registration into shorter steps and communicated the requirements more clearly.

Design & Testing

I built the final UI using the existing design system and created a clickable prototype covering the onboarding, browsing and registration journey.

I tested the prototype with 8 participants via Lyssna as qualitative usability testing, focusing on whether users understood the service, could navigate the new journey and knew what was required at key points. It highlighted two issues:

Delivery schedules — users weren't clear which days their local milk delivery person operated. I made the schedule explicit.

Password requirements — an animation intended to explain the requirements caused confusion. I replaced it with simpler, clearer feedback.

Clearer feedback

Outcome

The redesigned experience resulted in a 14% reduction in sign-up abandonment. More importantly, registration moved from being a barrier at the start of the experience to a natural step within the purchase journey.

What I Learnt

Users are more willing to commit when they've already experienced some value. This project showed me that when you ask users to register can be just as important as how you design the registration flow.
