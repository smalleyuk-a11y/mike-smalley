Euronics UK

One Platform for B2C and B2B

As the sole designer, I led the redesign of Euronics UK’s B2C store and B2B agent portal, replacing an old, hard-to-use site with a modern, on-brand platform. It brought purchasing from six disconnected systems down to one.

The Problem

Euronics UK’s site was dated, limited in functionality and off-brand, and purchasing was fragmented. It had to serve two very different audiences.

  • Agents (B2B): Time-poor retailers juggled six disconnected systems to place complex orders, from immediate and periodic deliveries to trade show offers. Invoices, credit control and claims all relied on support.
  • Customers (B2C): Products were difficult to search, filter and compare, and a slow, non-responsive mobile layout caused high cart abandonment.

Research & Insights

I led the design from discovery to launch, working alongside the Business Analyst and four engineers.

  • Discover: Stakeholder workshops, an audit of the six systems, analytics review and competitor review. The most common agent pain point was managing accounts and tracking processes across multiple platforms. Research showed B2C customers struggled to search, filter and compare, and a slow, non-responsive mobile layout caused high cart abandonment.
  • Define: Personas for agents and customers, and requirements set with the Business Analyst.

Ideation & Strategy

The aim was to create a MVP to give agents one fast, reliable place to order and manage their accounts, while giving shoppers a modern, easy-to-use store under one consistent brand.

Wireframe

  1. One platform instead of six. Agents were moving between six systems to complete everyday tasks. I designed a single gateway to the B2B section that brings together what those systems did separately, so agents start in one place.   2. An agent portal built for speed. Agents handle orders in volume, so I designed overview pages with dense, quick-edit tables to update many orders at once. For the MVP I kept the tables clear and functional rather than decorative, so agents could scan and act quickly.
  3. Self-service for agents. Agents previously relied on support for account tasks. They can now manage their own account details, order information, agent teams and permissions, budgets, invoices and credits, and claims.

  1. A fresh brand and responsive design. I used the Euronics brand guidelines and decided how to apply them across both the B2C and B2B experiences. I fully modernised the B2C layouts to make products easier to find and compare, and to improve the mobile experience, reusing components wherever possible to keep the system consistent and the build efficient.

Design & Handoff

  • Design: I mapped user flows, then built wireframes and responsive templates for desktop, tablet and mobile, supported by a design system in Sketch. SAP Commerce Cloud placed some limits on the design, which the engineers flagged, for example which areas could use dynamic text. I designed the B2C section first so the engineers could start building, then moved on to the B2B sections.
  • Test: I tested prototypes of both order flows in one round each: the B2C flow with 12 participants and the B2B order flow with 12. Both rounds ended in successful completion of the prototype tasks.
  • Build and launch: I handed over to the four engineers with documentation, reviewed the build through design QA and supported the release.

Outcome

  • Purchasing consolidated from 6 systems to 1.
  • Average time for an agent to place and track a complex order fell from 18 minutes (across multiple systems) to 6 minutes.
  • 78% of agents were actively using the new single portal within the first 6 weeks (measured by login + completed order).
  • Backend integration enabled automated order processing and scales for future growth.
  • Agents can manage their own invoices, credit and claims, reducing manual support work.
  • Customer support reported overwhelmingly positive feedback following the rollout.

What I Learnt

Aiming for an MVP meant putting function before polish. Simple tables and reused components got agents working in one place sooner, and I would use the same approach again.
